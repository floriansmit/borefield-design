Preset values, ranges & evidence

Conductivity values and observed sample ranges: Ditlefsen et al. (2014), GEUS Bulletin 31, Tables 1–2. Danish sample values are used as starting analogues for comparable Dutch lithologies, not as national Dutch measurements.

Cᵥ defaults are separate engineering estimates, calculated from an assumed porosity and solid/water volumetric heat capacity. They are not measured in that GEUS table. Peat and unsaturated Cᵥ values are illustrative. Ranges are not confidence intervals. Split layers at the water table; no flow-related conductivity bonus is applied. Drilling cost is your quote, not a lithology-derived value.

Pipe geometry, fluid, pumping & loop costs

Diameters are in mm. U spacing is the distance between opposite leg centres. Coaxial flow is down the inner pipe and up the annulus. Fluid defaults are illustrative bulk brine values, held constant; replace them with supplier values at your operating temperature. Flow is per borehole, with equal parallel circuits. Installed loop prices are per drilled metre, additional to drilling—exclude loops from your drilling quote to avoid double counting.

Methods & open source

What changes between engines?

Infinite line source: analytical conduction response E₁(r²/4αt)/(4πλ), with field-average interference. It has no finite-depth end effects.

pygfunction 2.3.1: actual Python package running locally through Pyodide 0.27.7 / SciPy in a background worker. Finite line sources with 8 axial segments per borehole and 1 m buried top depth. Choose uniform borehole wall temperature (UBWT) or uniform heat extraction rate (UHTR). Response factors are calculated on 96 log-spaced times plus the exact peak duration and interpolated logarithmically in time. No server is required for Python calculations.

All ground engines use length-weighted conductivity and heat capacity and depth-averaged undisturbed temperature. Calculated pipe mode uses actual pygfunction SingleUTube, MultipleUTube or Coaxial effective resistance, including the modelled axial thermal short-circuiting. That resistance is coupled to the field-average ground response; this is not a fully coupled segmented pipe/ground simulation. Groundwater flow and explicit geological contacts are not resolved. Optional regeneration is a specified monthly heat injection, subtracted from heating extraction before temporal superposition. Injection reverses the supply/return temperature ordering. Coincident heating and injection are represented by a monthly net load; regeneration is assumed off during the design heating peak. Injection equipment capacity and maximum allowable summer temperatures are not enforced. Additional regeneration electricity and initial capital cost are user inputs; no cooling benefit or cooling-system replacement is credited against gas. Field temperatures are lumped means, not the coldest individual pipe.

Heat pump, transient loads & economics

Monthly load steps are superimposed across every model year. COP and ground extraction Qground = Qheat(1 − 1/COP) are iterated together for every month until the COP residual is below 0.0001. A peak pulse is evaluated in the highest-demand month of each year; it checks the temperature constraint but is not counted twice in monthly energy demand. Months have equal duration (365.25 days / 12).

COP is an efficiency-scaled Carnot approximation with 5 K approach temperatures on both heat exchangers, bounded between 1.2 and 8. Heat-pump source temperature is the warm return from the ground; the limiting temperature is the cold supply from the heat pump to the ground. SPF includes calculated circuit pumping in pipe-geometry mode; the percentage allowance applies only in manual mode. Circuit pressure loss uses Darcy–Weisbach with a laminar annulus solution, a 2300–4000 Reynolds transition and a hydraulic-diameter turbulent approximation. Header/heat-pump pressure is entered separately; static elevation head is excluded for the closed loop. Pump electricity uses entered operating hours and efficiency; flow and fluid properties are constant. It replaces the percentage allowance. Pump heat is not added to useful heating output. Manufacturer maps and capacity/defrost/backup limits are not implemented.

Each year's predicted electricity is priced, escalated and discounted. Capital cost integrates drilling cost by depth plus per-borehole and fixed costs, and includes the specified heat-pump maintenance and replacement costs. Levelized heat cost divides discounted lifetime cost by discounted delivered heat. Optimization exhaustively checks only the stated count/depth grid. No site footprint or drilling-permit constraint is enforced.
